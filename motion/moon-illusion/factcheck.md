## Summary of flagged items

**❌ (blocks recording)**
1. **"closer by the width of the Earth itself"**: wrong. The overhead moon is closer by about one Earth *radius* (half the width). The 1.5% figure itself is correct (NASA). Reword: "Overhead, you're closer by about half the Earth's width, the distance from the surface to the centre, so on the horizon it's about one and a half percent farther away." Fix the diagram to match.

**⚠️ (reword/hedge)**
2. **Cleomedes "around two thousand years ago"**: he's dated c. AD 200, which is about 1,800 years ago (Ross 2019). Reword: "about eighteen hundred years ago… hazy air makes the low moon seem farther away."
3. **"Even NASA's answer starts with: we don't know"**: NASA's Ep. 50 answer opens with "We've been talking about this for 2,000 years." The admission ("We don't really know…", "no consensus yet") comes later. Reword: "Even a NASA scientist, asked this exact question, says: we don't really know." Card text: "WE DON'T REALLY KNOW".
4. **"four supermoons in a row"**: definition-dependent. Espenak/EarthSky and Farmers' Almanac count three (Nov, Dec, Jan); Feb 2027 qualifies only under Nolle's looser rule. Reword: "a run of supermoons: three or four in a row, depending on who's counting, starting with November's Beaver Moon."
