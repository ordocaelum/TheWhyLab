# Research: Why Your Body Makes You Feel Sick on Purpose

Organised by claim (RESEARCH.md §3). Tier 1 = reviews and peer-reviewed papers; tier 2 = reputable science news. Status: ✅ verified · ⚠️ hedged/contested · ❌ false or cut.

---

## C1: Sickness behaviour is an evolved, organised strategy, not just weakness
- Status: ✅
- Sources: [T1] Hart BL (1988) "Biological basis of the behavior of sick animals", *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 12(2):123–137. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3050629 (abstract: not "maladaptive… or the effect of debilitation" but "an organized, evolved behavioral strategy" to support fever against infection).
  [T1] Dantzer R et al. (2008) "From inflammation to sickness and depression: when the immune system subjugates the brain", *Nat Rev Neurosci* 9:46–56. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/2919277
- Nuance: Hart's work was mainly in mammals; some invertebrates show only part of the package (crickets: appetite loss but not reduced activity).

## C2: The symptom package: tiredness, more sleep, appetite and thirst loss, less grooming, withdrawal, low mood
- Status: ✅
- Sources: Hart 1988 (lethargy, depression, anorexia, reduced grooming); Dantzer 2008.
- Script wording: "exhausted, sleepy, not hungry, wanting to be alone, a little miserable".

## C3: Immune cells release cytokines (e.g. IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α) that tell the brain, via the blood and via the vagus nerve
- Status: ✅
- Sources: Dantzer 2008 (above); Dantzer & Kelley 2007 review, *Brain Behav Immun* (neural route via afferent vagus + humoral route).
- Visual anchor: immune cells as messengers; the vagus nerve as a "phone line" to the brain.

## C4: Fever is expensive: roughly 10% more energy per °C
- Status: ⚠️ hedged. The common "10–12.5%/°C" figure is widely repeated but weakly sourced; measured values in small studies range ~10–19%/°C.
- Sources: [T1] Discover Immunology (2023) review: "each degree increase… demands ~10% more metabolic resources". [T1] Cell Mol Immunol (2024) review: "10–12.5% increase in metabolic rate per °C". [T1] Evans SS, Repasky EA, Fisher DT (2015) "Fever and the thermal regulation of immunity", *Nat Rev Immunol* 15:335–349, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4786079/ (fever confers a survival benefit; the energetics figure is *not* confirmed in this paper).
- Script wording: "roughly ten percent more energy for every degree".

## C5: Cold-blooded desert iguanas "make" a fever by seeking heat when infected, and warmer lizards survived better
- Status: ✅ with a caveat
- Sources: [T1] Kluger MJ, Ringler DH, Anver MR (1975) "Fever and survival", *Science* 188:166–168. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1114347/ (Dipsosaurus dorsalis, Aeromonas hydrophila; ~2 °C behavioural fever; higher temp → higher survival).
  [T1] Bernheim et al. 1978: febrile lizards cleared bacteria faster.
- Nuance: lab study with injected bacteria; fever's adaptive value in cold-blooded animals is still debated → "in one famous experiment".

## C6: Sleep changes during infection appear tailored to support fever
- Status: ✅ (as a hypothesis by leading researchers)
- Sources: [T1] Imeri L, Opp MR (2009) "How (and why) the immune system makes us sleep", *Nat Rev Neurosci* 10:199–210. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2839418
- Script wording: "researchers think the extra sleep is tuned to help power the fever".

## C7: Appetite loss can be protective. In mice, feeding during a bacterial infection was deadly; during the flu, sugar helped
- Status: ✅ (mice only)
- Sources: [T1] Wang A et al., Medzhitov R (2016) "Opposing effects of fasting metabolism on tissue tolerance in bacterial and viral inflammation", *Cell* 166(6):1512–1525. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5555589 [T2] Yale News, Sep 2016.
- Nuance: glucose was the key nutrient; effects were about *tissue tolerance*, not killing germs. **Not dietary advice for humans.**

## C8: Ants exposed to a fungal pathogen change the colony's social network, increasing separation and protecting the queen and young
- Status: ✅
- Sources: [T1] Stroeymeyt N et al. (2018) "Social network plasticity decreases disease transmission in a eusocial insect", *Science* 362:941–945. https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/151636 [T2] ScienceDaily, Nov 2018.
- Nuance: the paper says "spatial and social segregation" by *both* exposed ants and healthy nestmates; don't say "self-isolate".

## C9: Wild vampire bats given an immune trigger (LPS) socially distanced
- Status: ✅
- Sources: [T1] Ripperger SP, Stockmaier S, Carter GG (2020), *Behavioral Ecology*. Data: https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.g4f4qrfnx [T2] ITV News, Oct 2020.
- Figure: in the first 6 h, a "sick" bat associated with ~4 fewer bats than controls.
- Nuance: LPS is a harmless bacterial fragment that triggers the immune response without infection. Claims about grooming or mating calls in press coverage are unverified → not used.

## C10: Healthy humans given a mild immune trigger (endotoxin) felt more socially disconnected and lower in mood
- Status: ✅
- Sources: [T1] Eisenberger NI et al. (2010), *Brain Behav Immun* 24. [T1] Moieni M et al. (2015) replication (n=115; stronger effects in women). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508216
- Nuance: short-term lab effect, measured by self-report.

## C11: When the immune signal doesn't switch off, it can contribute to depression; e.g. interferon-α therapy
- Status: ✅ (range)
- Sources: [T1] Dantzer 2008 (above). [T1] Raison CL et al. (2005) *J Clin Psychiatry* 66:41–48 (pegylated IFN-α). [T2] Psychiatric Times review: clinically relevant depression in 20–80% of IFN-α patients; ~40% on pegylated IFN-α + ribavirin in the first 6 months.
- Script wording: "in some studies, nearly half of patients".
- Nuance: depression has many causes; inflammation is one contributor in *some* people. Avoid implying all depression is inflammation.

## C12: Hart noticed that force-feeding sick livestock made them worse
- Status: ❌ cut. Only found in secondary retellings; not verified in the primary paper.

## C13: Safety framing
- Status: required. Fevers can become dangerous; the video is not medical advice. Script line: "Fever medicine isn't the enemy, and very high fevers need a doctor."

## C14: Honeybee colonies run a "hive fever" (~0.5 °C) when a fungal brood disease hits
- Status: ⚠️ hedged
- Sources: [T1] Starks PT, Blackie CA, Seeley TD (2000) *Naturwissenschaften* 87:229–231 (colonies raised brood-comb temperature ~0.5 °C in response to *Ascosphaera apis*; workers heat with flight muscles). [T2] Science News, 2000. [T1] Replication: *Insects* 2020, 11(8):528, https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4450/11/8/528 (the temperature rise was confirmed, but it was **not enough to prevent infection**).
- Script wording must include the replication caveat.

## C15: Strangers can spot an acutely sick person from a photo, a little better than chance
- Status: ✅ (modest effect)
- Sources: [T1] Axelsson J et al. (2018) "Identification of acutely sick people and facial cues of sickness", *Proc R Soc B* 285:20172430, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5784201 (endotoxin vs placebo; photos 2 h later; above-chance detection, ROC 0.62; cues: paler lips/skin, droopier mouth corners, hanging eyelids, more tired-looking).
- Nuance: modest accuracy, n=16 photographed. Script: "better than chance, though far from perfect".

## C16: Fever is a reset thermostat: cytokines → prostaglandin E2 → hypothalamus raises the target temperature; the body then shivers, cuts heat loss
- Status: ✅ (textbook; routes into the brain partly debated)
- Sources: [T1] Evans et al. 2015 (above); [T1] 2018 *Neuroscientist* review on PGE2/EP3 in the preoptic hypothalamus, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1073858418760481 ; [T1] PMC7122269.
- Script wording: "a chemical messenger"; don't detail which route crosses into the brain.

## C17: Fish can't make internal heat either, but infected fish swim to warmer water; mild fever helped them clear infection and repair tissue
- Status: ✅ (species not confirmed in my sources → "fish")
- Sources: [T1] Haddad F, Soliman AM, et al. (2023) *eLife* 12:e83644, https://elifesciences.org/articles/83644 ; [T2] ScienceDaily, Mar 2023.
