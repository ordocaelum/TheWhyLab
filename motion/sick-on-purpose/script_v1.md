## COLD OPEN *(Alarm → Dusk)*

You wake up and something is wrong. [op]
*A tired human in bed, morning light through blinds. Hook scene.*

Your head is heavy. Your whole body aches. Breakfast sounds disgusting, and the idea of answering a single text message feels like climbing a mountain. [op]
*Phone buzzing; food icons turning green-grey.*

You probably blame the germ. It got inside you, it's breaking things, so of course you feel awful. [op]
*A germ character appears on the bedside table.*

But here's the strange part. Much of what makes you *feel* sick isn't the germ at all. [C1]
*The germ shrugs innocently; the camera pans to the human's head, where the brain lights up.*

It's you. Your own body is doing this to you. On purpose. [C1]
*Title card: SICK ON PURPOSE.*

## CHAPTER 1: THE ALARM *(Lab)*

Let's zoom in. A few bacteria slip into a tiny cut on your hand. Your immune cells quickly notice the intruders. [C3]
*Zoom-in transition through the skin; cute but mischievous bacteria rods.*

The first responders don't just fight. They also start sending messages: tiny proteins called cytokines. [C3]
*Our mascot, a round blob-shaped immune cell, releases glowing envelope cytokines.*

Those messages spread through your bloodstream. And some take a shortcut, along the vagus nerve, a long cable that runs from your gut and organs straight up into your brain. [C3]
*Top-down map: the body as a city, the vagus nerve drawing on as a glowing cable.*

When the message arrives, your brain does two big things. [C16][C1]

First, it resets your thermostat. A chemical messenger tells a small region deep in your brain, the hypothalamus, to aim for a higher temperature. [C16]
*Cutaway: a thermostat dial inside the brain turns from 37 to 39.*

So even though you're already warm, you suddenly feel cold. You shiver. You pull the blanket tighter. Your body is cranking up the heat to hit its new target. [C16]
*The human shivers under a blanket; the internal furnace roars.*

And second, it changes *you*. [C1][C2]
*Brain "control room" with switches labelled ENERGY, SLEEP, HUNGER, SOCIAL, MOOD.*

You get tired. You want to sleep. You lose your appetite. You stop caring how you look. You want to be left alone. You feel a bit miserable. [C2]
*Each line a quick beat: a switch flips, the human reacts.*

Scientists have a name for this whole package: sickness behaviour. [C1]
*Big label stamp: SICKNESS BEHAVIOUR.*

For a long time, people assumed it was just weakness, a body breaking down under attack. Then, in 1988, a veterinarian named Benjamin Hart looked at it differently. [C1]
*Hart as a simple illustrated vet character, with sick animals around him: a dog, a cow, a horse.*

Sick animals, he argued, aren't failing. They're following a plan. An organised, evolved strategy, all built around one weapon. [C1]
*Verdict scene: "WEAKNESS" crossed out, "STRATEGY" stamped in.*

## CHAPTER 2: THE FURNACE *(Alarm)*

That weapon is fever. [C1]
*Thermometer rises; the backdrop shifts to the Alarm palette.*

Turning up the heat makes life harder for many invaders, and it speeds up parts of your immune response. [C4]
*Immune cells move faster in the heat while bacteria sweat and slow down.*

But fever is incredibly expensive. Every extra degree costs your body roughly ten percent more energy, maybe more. [C4]
*A battery icon draining; "+10% PER °C" counts up.*

So where does all that energy come from? From everything else you'd normally be doing. [C1][C2]
*The human's daily activities (gym, cooking, chatting) dim out one by one, their energy flowing into a furnace.*

Walking around burns energy, so you feel exhausted. Losing heat wastes energy, so you curl up into a ball. [C2]
*The human curls up; a heat-conserving posture.*

Even your sleep changes. Researchers think the extra sleep you get when you're sick is tuned to help power the fever. [C6]
*Night scene; sleep "Z"s flow into the furnace.*

And this strategy is old. Really, really old. [C5]
*Timeline zooming back through evolution.*

In one famous experiment, scientists infected desert iguanas with bacteria. Lizards are cold-blooded. They can't make a fever from the inside. [C5]
*Our cute desert iguana in a terrarium with a heat lamp.*

So they made one from the outside. The infected lizards crawled toward the heat, and raised their body temperature by about two degrees. [C5]
*The iguana scoots under the lamp; a thermometer climbs +2 °C.*

When researchers held infected lizards at cooler temperatures, more of them died. The ones kept warmer survived better. [C5]
*Split screen: cool chamber vs warm chamber; survival bars.*

Fish do it too. A 2023 study found that infected fish deliberately swim into warmer water, and that a mild fever helped them clear the infection and repair damaged tissue. [C17]
*A swim tank with a temperature gradient, blue to orange; a sick fish drifts to the warm end.*

Even honeybees have tried a version of it. When a fungus attacks their young, worker bees vibrate their flight muscles and heat the nursery comb by about half a degree. [C14]
*A cutaway hive: bees buzzing, warm glow over the brood comb.*

Although, to be fair to the fungus, a later study found that tiny hive fever wasn't enough to stop the infection on its own. Evolution doesn't always win. [C14]
*A fungus character gives a smug wave; the bees look deflated.*

## CHAPTER 3: THE HUNGER STRIKE *(Jungle)*

Now for the weirdest symptom of all: you stop wanting to eat. [C2]
*A plate of food; the human pushes it away.*

That seems backwards. Surely you need fuel to fight? [op]

In 2016, a team at Yale tested this in mice. Mice with a bacterial infection naturally stopped eating. When researchers fed them anyway, far more of them died. [C7]
*Lab mice characters; a feeding tube; the survival chart drops.*

But here's the twist. With a viral infection, the flu, it flipped. Feeding those mice actually helped them survive, and blocking their use of sugar was deadly. [C7]
*The chart mirrors: flu mice with sugar cubes, survival rises.*

It wasn't mainly about starving the germ. What changed was how well the mouse's own tissues could handle the damage. Different invaders, different fuel strategy. [C7]
*Two tissue shields: one powered by fasting, one by glucose.*

Now, these were mice, not people, so this is not a diet plan. But it hints that losing your appetite isn't necessarily a malfunction. It might be a setting. [C7]
*Caution card: "MICE ≠ MEDICAL ADVICE". Then a dial labelled APPETITE.*

## CHAPTER 4: THE WALL *(Verdict → Dusk)*

And then there's the loneliness. [C2]
*The human alone in a dim room; phone buzzing, ignored.*

When you're sick, everyone feels far away. You don't want to see anyone, and you might feel a little low. [C10]

Scientists tested this directly. They gave healthy volunteers a tiny dose of a harmless bacterial fragment, enough to trip the immune alarm without a real infection. [C10]
*Clinic scene, a volunteer with an IV; the immune alarm lights flash.*

Within hours, the volunteers reported feeling more disconnected from other people, and their mood dropped. [C10]
*A meter: CONNECTED → DISCONNECTED.*

And it works in the other direction too. In another study, people photographed two hours after getting that same immune trigger were picked out as "sick" by strangers, better than chance, though far from perfectly. [C15]
*Photo-lineup scene: faces with paler lips, droopy eyelids; a stranger pointing.*

Pale lips, droopy eyelids, a tired mouth. Your face may quietly broadcast a warning: maybe keep your distance. [C15][op]

Animals do the same. Wild vampire bats given a similar immune trigger spent less time near their roost-mates. In the first six hours, each sick bat hung out with about four fewer bats than healthy ones. [C9]
*A cave and a cute bat colony; one bat drifts to the edge as the dotted lines between bats fade.*

And ants take it even further. When some workers in a colony were exposed to a deadly fungus, the whole colony reorganised itself. Exposed ants and their healthy nestmates spread apart, keeping the danger away from the queen and the young. [C8]
*Top-down ant colony map; ant dots rearrange into separate zones; the queen protected in the centre.*

So wanting to be alone isn't you being antisocial. It may be your body building a wall around everyone you care about. [C8][C9][C10][op]
*Wall scene: a glowing barrier around the sick human, family safe outside, waving.*

## CHAPTER 5: WHEN THE SWITCH GETS STUCK *(Deep space → Dusk)*

But there's a darker side to all this. [C11]

Sickness behaviour is meant to be temporary. Germ gone, signal off, and you're back to your normal self. [C11]
*The switchboard flips back to ON.*

But sometimes the immune signal doesn't switch off. In long-term illnesses, or during certain medical treatments, the alarm keeps ringing. [C11]
*One switch stuck; a warning light blinks.*

One example is a drug called interferon-alpha, which boosts the immune system against some infections and cancers. In some studies, nearly half of the patients taking it developed symptoms of depression, which is why doctors watch for it. [C11]
*A medicine vial icon; half of a row of figures turns grey-blue.*

That doesn't mean all depression comes from inflammation. Depression has many causes. But for some people, it seems the body's sickness program can get stuck in the "on" position. [C11]
*Many-roads diagram: one road labelled INFLAMMATION among many.*

## OUTRO *(Dusk)*

So the next time you're lying in bed, aching, shivering, not hungry, ignoring every message, remember this. [op]
*Back to the opening bedroom shot, now warm and golden.*

You're not falling apart. You're running a strategy that shows up again and again in nature: in lizards, fish, bats, ants, and you. [C1][C5][C8][C9][C17]
*The family of species lines up: iguana, fish, bat, ant, human, each with a tiny blanket.*

That doesn't mean fever medicine is the enemy. Very high or long-lasting fevers can be dangerous, and so can any fever in a baby. That's when you call a doctor. [C13]
*A doctor character gives a thumbs up.*

But it does mean that rest isn't laziness. It's strategy. [op]
*End card: the human sleeps peacefully; the immune cells give a thumbs up.*

So, next time you're sick: do you fight through it, or do you let your body do its thing? [op]
*Question card, comment bubble.*
