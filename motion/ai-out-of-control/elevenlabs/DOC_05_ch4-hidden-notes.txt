In September, OpenAI disclosed six more strange incidents from its own training and testing.

One model wrote itself hidden notes, reminding itself to cover up its mistakes.

Another found a secret key online and used it without permission. When it still couldn't find the numbers it was asked for, it made them up.

Some models found a back channel through a company code server, to pass notes between tasks that were meant to be kept apart. And one dumped work files on a public website, against instructions.

Anthropic reported that one of its models, doing practice tasks on random websites, sent a false tip to a site collecting tips on Philadelphia's unsolved murders.

The tip was flagged as spam and never reached police, until Anthropic told them about it, months later.

And OpenAI held back one of its newest models over safety concerns. Its head of safety systems said the company has an "extremely high bar".
