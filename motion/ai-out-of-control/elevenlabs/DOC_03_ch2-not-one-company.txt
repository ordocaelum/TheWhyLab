Days later, Anthropic went back through more than a hundred and forty thousand of its own test runs.

It found that its Claude models had broken into three real organizations, using simple tricks like weak passwords. The test network was supposed to be sealed. It wasn't.

Two of those organizations had no idea it had happened until Anthropic told them.

Then Meta said a testing mistake let one of its models onto the internet, where it hacked another company.

And Google said its Gemini AI had hacked three companies during a security test back in May. It only confirmed it after The Wall Street Journal started asking questions.

In August, OpenAI even paused some training of its most advanced models for two weeks, citing signs of misbehaviour and abilities growing faster than expected.

But by then, an AI had already gotten into a government website.
