## Most important fixes (in priority order)

1. **Transluce / Canada attribution (line 56): defamation risk.** Transluce said it "could not confidently link" the Canadian attempts to OpenAI. Only tie the Education Department attempt to "agents that appeared to come from OpenAI", and add "OpenAI says it's reviewing the findings."
2. **Albanese quote (line 50).** The English phrase "didn't accept no for an answer" turns up only in secondary aggregators and a Spanish translation. Confirm it in the pm.gov.au transcript, or switch to "found a way around those blocks".
3. **"Called the delay unacceptable" (line 54).** He called the *situation* "obviously unacceptable" and said OpenAI took "way too long".
4. **"Sandbox that wasn't sealed" (line 30).** In OpenAI's case the models exploited an unknown flaw in the one tool they were allowed to use. Say "found a hidden flaw in their sandbox". (For Anthropic, "supposed to be sealed, it wasn't" is correct.)
5. **Meta "by itself" (line 40).** A testing misconfiguration put the model online. Use "a testing mistake let one of its models onto the internet".
6. **"Passwords" (lines 11, 26).** Hugging Face says *credentials* (cloud and cluster keys). Use "login keys" or "credentials".
7. **Line 68, the public-websites back channel.** The cross-task notes went through OpenAI's *internal* code repository. Reword as given.
8. **Line 80: shutdown sabotage wasn't in the "fake office" tests.** That was Palisade's separate maths-task setup.
9. **Line 82: don't attribute the counter-claim to Google DeepMind.** No primary source was found. Use the generic wording and cite the Mar 2026 arXiv paper plus Anthropic's own caveat.
10. **Line 72: the OpenAI pause was in August.** It was a two-week pause of some training, citing misalignment signs. Don't imply it came after the September disclosures.
11. Smaller fixes: "hide its errors **from users**" (64), "**led** by 100 experts" (92), "scams" and "probably higher" (94), "safety rails switched off" on every incident (84), "went after a government" (44).

Date check (publishing on or soon after 2026-10-29): "This summer" (ok; "In July" is better), "In July", "In June", "In September" are all fine; "this year" (IASR, Feb 2026) and "Last year" (FBI 2025) both expire on Jan 1 2027. Recheck A5–A9 and A18 on publish day; this story is moving weekly.
