For decades, you were told the answer was simple. Rinse it. Sort it. Recycle it.

An NPR investigation found industry documents from as far back as the 1970s doubting that plastic recycling could ever pay off on a large scale. The industry says it never intentionally misled anyone.

In 2024, California's attorney general sued ExxonMobil, one of the world's biggest plastic producers, accusing it of deceiving the public about recycling for decades.

Exxon denies it, and says its advanced recycling works. The case isn't over.

And production isn't slowing down. Without new rules, the OECD projects the world could use almost three times as much plastic by 2060, with less than a fifth of the waste recycled.

But just as public fear reached its peak, the science itself started to crack.
