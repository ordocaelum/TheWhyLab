Right now, by one scientist's estimate, your brain could hold about a plastic spoon's worth of plastic.

That estimate comes from a 2025 study that analysed brain tissue from people who had died, and reported far more tiny plastic than anyone expected.

They found it in blood. In the arteries of patients who then had more heart attacks and strokes. In the water you drink.

And the plastic industry is accused of knowing, for decades, that recycling would never solve the problem. It denies that.

But there's a twist. A growing number of scientists now say some of these terrifying numbers might be wrong. And that could be even worse.
