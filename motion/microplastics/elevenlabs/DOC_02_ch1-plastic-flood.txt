To understand how plastic got inside you, start with how much of it there is.

In twenty years, the world doubled its plastic production, to around four hundred and sixty million tonnes a year.

And only about nine percent of all that plastic waste actually gets recycled. The rest is burned, buried, or dumped.

Here's the problem. Plastic doesn't really go away. It just gets smaller.

Sun, waves and heat break it into microplastics, smaller than a grain of rice, and then into nanoplastics, far thinner than a human hair.

Small enough to slip into places plastic was never supposed to go.

And it isn't only bottles. Every time you drive, your tyres shed plastic dust onto the road. Every time you wash a fleece, it sheds tiny fibres.

One 2017 estimate found that washing synthetic clothes and wearing down tyres cause almost two thirds of the plastic that reaches the ocean already as tiny particles.

In 2024, one study looked at bottled water with a powerful new microscope technique.

It found an average of about two hundred and forty thousand plastic particles in a single litre. Most of them were nanoplastics.

And it doesn't stay in the bottle.
