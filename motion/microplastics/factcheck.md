# Fact-check: microplastics script v1

Independent pass (RESEARCH.md step 6), 2026-10-25. Each claim was checked against primary or tier-1/2 sources, not against research.md. Some sources (Guardian, NEJM, Nature Medicine, PubMed) blocked direct fetch, so those claims were confirmed through syndicated copies, PMC or abstract-level coverage. This is noted where it applies.

Legend: ✅ OK as written · ⚠️ reword or hedge · ❌ wrong, blocks recording.

**Result: 0 ❌, 17 ⚠️.** None of the problems is a wrong number. They are about framing, harm and fairness: cold-open hedging, legal and defamation framing, the "draft" status of the EPA list, and fairness to the original researchers.

## Summary of every ⚠️ (no ❌)

1. **L7 cold open, "a spoonful of plastic inside your skull".** This is the press framing of one researcher's extrapolation (~7 g compared with a ~5 g spoon), and it is contested. Proposed: "Right now, by one scientist's estimate, your brain could hold about a plastic spoon's worth of plastic."
2. **L9, "Not a figure of speech. In 2025, scientists cut into human brains…".** The samples were autopsy tissue, and the spoon is a comparison. Proposed: "In 2025, scientists analysed brain tissue from people who had died, and reported so much tiny plastic that, by one estimate, it would weigh about as much as a plastic spoon."
3. **L11, "people who later had heart attacks and strokes".** 20% of patients with plastic had an event, against 7.5% without. Proposed: "In the arteries of patients who then had more heart attacks and strokes."
4. **L13, "the companies selling it to you are accused…".** This is a broad accusation with no denial. Proposed: "And the plastic industry is accused of knowing, for decades, that recycling would never solve the problem. It denies that."
5. **L33, the IUCN two-thirds figure.** It applies only to *primary* microplastics. Proposed: "One 2017 estimate found that washing synthetic clothes and wearing down tyres cause almost two thirds of the plastic that reaches the ocean already as tiny particles."
6. **L51, "enough to make cardiologists very nervous".** No source. Proposed: "But it set off alarm bells around the world." [op], or cut.
7. **L68, NPR.** Add what the documents show and the industry's denial. Proposed: "An NPR investigation found industry documents from as far back as the 1970s doubting that plastic recycling could ever pay off on a large scale. The industry says it never intentionally misled anyone."
8. **L84, "brain is about sixty percent fat".** The 60% is dry weight. Proposed: "Take out the water, and the brain is about sixty percent fat."
9. **L87, "A review found eighteen studies".** It was an analysis (Rauert 2025), not a review. Proposed: "One analysis listed eighteen studies that didn't properly account for these false positives."
10. **L90, "their methods hold up".** This overstates the researchers' position; Campen also conceded room for improvement. Proposed: "The original researchers push back. They say the critics are speculating without data of their own, though they admit their methods can still be improved."
11. **L94, "often skipped".** Proposed: "Critics say quite a few papers, in the rush to publish, skipped basic lab checks, like running blank samples to catch contamination." The Guardian notes there is no suggestion of malpractice.
12. **L100, "Even the harshest critics agree".** Proposed: "Even one of the harshest critics says it's safe to assume plastic is inside us. The fight is over how much, and what it's doing."
13. **L105, "plastic rarely travels alone. It carries… BPA and phthalates".** Proposed: "And many plastics contain added chemicals, like BPA and phthalates, which pediatricians warn can interfere with hormones."
14. **L107, the EPA list.** It is a draft only; the final CCL 6 is expected by about Nov 17 2026. Proposed: "The EPA has, for the first time, proposed adding microplastics to its list of drinking water contaminants it may one day regulate." Switch back to "has put" only if the final list is published before release.
15. **L111, the treaty "meant to cut plastic production".** Proposed: "And the global treaty meant to end plastic pollution is still stuck, with countries split over whether to limit production."
16. **L117, "The flawed studies".** Proposed: "Shaky studies could even help the plastic industry wave the whole problem away."
17. **L119, "plastic is in your blood, your arteries and your water".** Proposed: "But plastic is almost certainly inside you, it's in the water you drink, and production keeps climbing."

## Research-file corrections
- M6: research.md gives 58–60% of plaques. The figure is 58.4% of patients (150/257) with polyethylene.
- M9: Rauert 2025 is a methods paper in ES&T, not a review. The count of 18 studies comes from Guardian coverage of it.
- M10: the blood-study quote is from **Marja Lamoree**, the senior author, not Leslie. Exact wording: "could be maybe twice lower, or 10 times higher."
- M7: The Transmitter reported duplicated images in the brain paper, and an Author Correction was issued (DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-03675-x). This belongs in the counter-evidence section.
- M15: the draft list was published in the Federal Register on Apr 6 2026 (announced Apr 2); comments closed Jun 5.
