# Research: There's Plastic in Your Brain. Or Is There?

Slug: `microplastics` · long-form ~9 min · narrator-only (Doc's voice, Infographics-style flow) · Thumbnail: **IT'S IN YOU**

## Brief
- **Question:** Is there really plastic in your brain, and who's to blame?
- **Turn (midpoint):** the scariest numbers may be partly measurement errors (fat mimics plastic in a common test). But plastics in the body are real, the harm is plausible, and the recycling story people were sold is under legal attack.
- **Stakes:** your brain, your heart, your water. **Villain:** an industry accused of selling recycling it knew wouldn't work. **Escalation:** bottle → blood → arteries → brain → lawsuit → the science cracks → what's still true.
- **Score (new criteria):** conflict 5, viewer stakes 5, escalation 5, surprise 5, evidence 4, demand 5.

Tiers per RESEARCH.md.

## M1: Global plastic production doubled from 234 Mt (2000) to 460 Mt (2019)
- ✅ [T1] OECD Global Plastics Outlook (Feb 2022). Script: "doubled in about twenty years, to around four hundred and sixty million tonnes a year".

## M2: Only ~9% of plastic waste is recycled (19% burned, ~50% landfill, 22% dumped/leaked)
- ✅ [T1] OECD Global Plastics Outlook (2022), 2019 data. Script: "about nine percent".

## M3: Plastic doesn't biodegrade; it breaks into microplastics (<5 mm) and nanoplastics (<1 µm)
- ✅ [T1] NOAA microplastics definition (<5 mm); [T1] PNAS 2024 (nanoplastics <1 µm). Visual anchor: a human hair is ~70 µm.

## M4: Bottled water: average ~240,000 plastic particles per litre (range 110,000–400,000), ~90% nanoplastics
- ✅ [T1] Qian et al., PNAS (Jan 2024), Columbia/Rutgers; 3 brands, 5 bottles each. [T2] AP/PBS. Industry (IBWA) says no standard methods and no consensus on harm.
- ⚠️ This number is also challenged (see M10); script says "one study found".

## M5: Plastic in human blood: 17 of 22 healthy donors (77%)
- ✅ [T1] Leslie et al., Environment International (Mar 2022), Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam. [T2] Smithsonian. Authors called it a first step; small sample.

## M6: Plastic in artery plaque linked to ~4.5× higher risk of heart attack, stroke or death
- ✅ [T1] Marfella et al., NEJM (Mar 2024): 304 enrolled, 257 followed ~34 months; particles in ~58–60% of plaques; HR 4.53 (95% CI 2.00–10.27). Observational, can't prove cause. [T2] Healio, ScienceAlert.
- Script: "about four and a half times more likely... the study can't prove the plastic caused it."

## M7: Brains: Nihart et al., Nature Medicine (2025): brain tissue held 7–30× more than liver/kidney; ~50% higher in 2024 than 2016; more in brains with dementia; widely reported as "about a spoon's worth"
- ✅/⚠️ [T1] Nihart et al., Nat Med 2025 (doi 10.1038/s41591-024-03453-1). [T2] Science Media Centre; EHN. Causality not established; reverse causation possible (damaged brains clear less).
- ⚠️ "Spoon" is a press framing (~7 g); script: "roughly the weight of a plastic spoon, by one estimate". Challenged (M10).

## M8: Recycling: NPR (2020) reported industry officials long knew large-scale recycling would probably never work; California's AG sued ExxonMobil (Sept 2024) alleging it deceived the public; Exxon denies ("advanced recycling works")
- ✅ [T2] NPR, "California sues ExxonMobil for misleading public on plastic recycling" (Sept 23 2024) + NPR/Frontline 2020 investigation. Allegation, not a verdict: script says "accuses", includes the denial.

## M9: Py-GC/MS (common method) can mistake fats for polyethylene/PVC → false positives; the brain is ~60% fat
- ✅ [T2] Mother Jones (Jan 2026); [T2] Guardian coverage; [T1] Rauert et al. (Jan 2025): 18 studies didn't properly account for false positives; Py-GC/MS "not currently suitable" for PE/PVC.

## M10: Seven high-profile studies have been formally challenged (incl. brain, plaque, blood, bottled water); a "Matters arising" letter cites limited contamination controls in the brain study; critics suggest rising obesity could explain the brain trend
- ✅ [T2] Mother Jones (Jan 2026); [T2] Fortune (Feb 2026). Authors dispute (Campen: criticism "conjectural"; blood-study author: "convinced we detected microplastics", amount uncertain).

## M11: Even critics agree plastics are in us. The dispute is how much, and the harm
- ✅ [T2] Mother Jones (critic: "We do have plastics in us").

## M12: Critics warn shaky studies can mislead policy *and* help industry dismiss real risks
- ✅ [T2] Mother Jones (Jan 2026).

## M13: Clinics advertise blood-"cleaning" treatments for microplastics costing thousands (~£10,000)
- ✅ [T2] Mother Jones/Guardian (2026). Script: "some clinics now charge thousands", no medical claim.

## M14: Oct 14 2026, Nature (Columbia + UC Berkeley): micro/nanoplastics in water generate hydroxyl radicals, a possible pathway to cell damage; not inert
- ✅ [T2] Columbia News / Columbia Climate School (Oct 14 2026); [T2] ScienceAlert. Lab finding; harm to people not shown. Script: "a possible way they could do damage".

## M15: EPA's draft Contaminant Candidate List 6 (Apr 2, 2026) includes microplastics for the first time; not regulation; final expected by Nov 17 2026
- ✅ [T1] EPA news release (2026); [T2] Morgan Lewis, Food Safety Magazine. Later EPA declined to add microplastics to UCMR 6 monitoring (Holland & Knight, Jul 2026).

## M16: HHS committed $144 million to study microplastics' health risks (2026)
- ✅ [T2] Columbia News; reporting on HHS/RFK Jr. announcement. Script: "a hundred and forty-four million dollars to study it".

## M17: Global plastics treaty talks remain deadlocked
- ✅ [T2] multiple (2025–2026 coverage). Script: "a global treaty to cut plastic is still stuck".

## M18: Pediatricians (AAP): avoid microwaving food/drinks in plastic when possible; try to keep plastics out of the dishwasher (heat releases chemicals like BPA and phthalates)
- ✅ [T1] AAP / HealthyChildren.org, "Food Additives and Child Health" (2018). Only precaution offered; no medical advice beyond it.

## M19: Primary microplastics are ~15–31% of the ~9.5 Mt of plastic entering oceans yearly; almost two-thirds come from synthetic-textile washing and tyre abrasion
- ✅ [T1] IUCN, Boucher & Friot, "Primary Microplastics in the Oceans" (2017) + IUCN press release. (Avoid the contested 35%/28% split; a 2025 UNU estimate puts textiles lower.)

## M20: Microplastics found in 4 of 6 human placentas (12 fragments)
- ✅ [T1] Ragusa et al., "Plasticenta", Environment International (2021). Pilot study, n=6.

## M21: OECD baseline: plastics use 460 Mt (2019) → 1,231 Mt (2060), almost triple; recycled share only rises to ~17%
- ✅ [T1] OECD Global Plastics Outlook: Policy Scenarios to 2060 (2022). No-new-policy scenario.

## Fact-check corrections (factcheck.md, 2026-10-25): 0 ❌, 17 ⚠️ reworded
- M7: "spoon" is senior author Campen's extrapolation (~7 g), contested ("biologically implausible", Rauert); the paper received an Author Correction over duplicated images (The Transmitter).
- M6: 58.4% = share of patients with detectable plastic (not plaques); events 20% vs 7.5%.
- M9: Rauert et al. 2025 is a methods paper, not a review. Brain ~60% fat = dry weight.
- M10: the "twice lower, or 10 times higher" quote is Marja Lamoree (senior author), not Leslie. Campen: critiques "conjectural", but conceded room for improvement. Bottled-water challenge = Materić PNAS letter on blanks (Nov 2024).
- M8: NPR documents date to the 1970s; industry says it never intentionally misled anyone. Exxon also sued AG Bonta for defamation (federal judge in Texas let it proceed, Feb 2026). No ruling in the SF case as of Oct 2026. Recheck at publish.
- M15: only the draft CCL 6 (Apr 2026) exists; final due ~Nov 17 2026. Recheck at publish.
- "This month" changed to "in October" (Nature, Oct 14 2026).
