# Script v1: There's Plastic in Your Brain. Or Is There?

Format: narrator only (DOC voice, Infographics-style). `[Mn]` = claim in research.md, `[op]` = narration/opinion. Hook lines marked ⚡ (one every ~30–40 s). Target ~9 min.

## COLD OPEN

DOC: Right now, by one scientist's estimate, your brain could hold about a plastic spoon's worth of plastic. [M7]
  > SCENE: slow push into a human head silhouette; a glowing plastic spoon materialises inside
DOC: That estimate comes from a 2025 study that analysed brain tissue from people who had died, and reported far more tiny plastic than anyone expected. [M7]
  > SCENE: lab, brain on a tray, particles light up; tag "NATURE MEDICINE, 2025"
DOC: They found it in blood. In the arteries of patients who then had more heart attacks and strokes. In the water you drink. [M5, M6, M4]
  > SCENE: three fast push-ins: blood vessel, artery plaque, water bottle, each with glowing particles
DOC: And the plastic industry is accused of knowing, for decades, that recycling would never solve the problem. It denies that. [M8]
  > SCENE: shadowy boardroom, recycling logo cracks
DOC: ⚡ But there's a twist. A growing number of scientists now say some of these terrifying numbers might be wrong. And that could be even worse. [M10]
  > SCENE: data screen glitches, red "ERROR"; title card IT'S IN YOU

## CHAPTER 1: THE PLASTIC FLOOD

DOC: To understand how plastic got inside you, start with how much of it there is. [op]
  > SCENE: stat card, mountains of plastic
DOC: In twenty years, the world doubled its plastic production, to around four hundred and sixty million tonnes a year. [M1]
  > SCENE: counter racing up, 234 → 460 Mt, stack of bottles towering past skyscrapers
DOC: And only about nine percent of all that plastic waste actually gets recycled. The rest is burned, buried, or dumped. [M2]
  > SCENE: pie chart: 9% recycled glowing tiny; landfill, fire, ocean
DOC: ⚡ Here's the problem. Plastic doesn't really go away. It just gets smaller. [M3]
  > SCENE: a bottle cracking into fragments, fragments into dust
DOC: Sun, waves and heat break it into microplastics, smaller than a grain of rice, and then into nanoplastics, far thinner than a human hair. [M3]
  > SCENE: zoom scale: bottle → rice grain → hair → nanoparticle
DOC: Small enough to slip into places plastic was never supposed to go. [op]
DOC: And it isn't only bottles. Every time you drive, your tyres shed plastic dust onto the road. Every time you wash a fleece, it sheds tiny fibres. [M19]
  > SCENE: car tyre spinning, dust trail; washing machine drum, fibres swirling out
DOC: One 2017 estimate found that washing synthetic clothes and wearing down tyres cause almost two thirds of the plastic that reaches the ocean already as tiny particles. [M19]
  > SCENE: ocean cross-section, fibres and dust drifting down
DOC: In 2024, one study looked at bottled water with a powerful new microscope technique. [M4]
  > SCENE: lab, laser microscope on a bottle
DOC: It found an average of about two hundred and forty thousand plastic particles in a single litre. Most of them were nanoplastics. [M4]
  > SCENE: bottle fills with glowing specks, counter 240,000
DOC: ⚡ And it doesn't stay in the bottle. [op]

## CHAPTER 2: INSIDE YOU

DOC: In 2022, researchers in Amsterdam tested the blood of twenty two healthy adults. [M5]
  > SCENE: 22 avatars in a row, blood vials
DOC: Seventeen of them had plastic in their blood. [M5]
  > SCENE: 17 vials light up red
DOC: Then doctors in Italy looked inside the clogged neck arteries of patients having surgery. [M6]
  > SCENE: artery cross-section, plaque, push in
DOC: Most of the plaques had micro or nanoplastics in them. And over the next three years, those patients were about four and a half times more likely to have a heart attack, a stroke, or die. [M6]
  > SCENE: timeline 34 months, heart-attack and stroke icons; bar 4.5×
DOC: The study couldn't prove the plastic caused it. But it set off alarm bells around the world. [M6, op]
DOC: It's even turned up in the placenta, the organ that feeds a baby before it's born. A small Italian study found plastic in four of the six placentas it tested. [M20]
  > SCENE: soft-lit womb silhouette, placenta glowing, 4 of 6 markers
DOC: ⚡ And then, the brain. [M7]
  > SCENE: camera pushes into a skull, brain glows
DOC: The 2025 study compared brains from people who died in 2016 with brains from people who died in 2024. [M7]
  > SCENE: two brains labelled 2016 and 2024
DOC: The newer brains held about fifty percent more plastic. Brains had far more than livers or kidneys. And the brains of people with dementia had even more. [M7]
  > SCENE: bar charts rising; dementia brain glowing brightest
DOC: Again, that doesn't prove plastic causes dementia. A damaged brain might simply be worse at clearing it out. [M7]
  > SCENE: arrows looping both ways: PLASTIC → DAMAGE? / DAMAGE → PLASTIC?
DOC: ⚡ But if plastic is everywhere, inside everyone, somebody had to let it get this far. [op]

## CHAPTER 3: THE RECYCLING PROMISE

DOC: For decades, you were told the answer was simple. Rinse it. Sort it. Recycle it. [op]
  > SCENE: kitchen, avatar rinsing a bottle, recycling bin glowing
DOC: An NPR investigation found industry documents from as far back as the 1970s doubting that plastic recycling could ever pay off on a large scale. The industry says it never intentionally misled anyone. [M8]
  > SCENE: old memos sliding across a desk, highlighted lines (generic, no real text)
DOC: In 2024, California's attorney general sued ExxonMobil, one of the world's biggest plastic producers, accusing it of deceiving the public about recycling for decades. [M8]
  > SCENE: courthouse, lawsuit stamp; tag "ACCUSED, NOT PROVEN"
DOC: Exxon denies it, and says its advanced recycling works. The case isn't over. [M8]
  > SCENE: corporate spokesperson silhouette at a podium
DOC: And production isn't slowing down. Without new rules, the OECD projects the world could use almost three times as much plastic by 2060, with less than a fifth of the waste recycled. [M21]
  > SCENE: line chart climbing off the screen to 2060; recycling sliver stays thin
DOC: ⚡ But just as public fear reached its peak, the science itself started to crack. [M9]

## CHAPTER 4: THE TWIST

DOC: Here's how many of these studies measured plastic. They heat a tissue sample until it breaks apart, then read the chemical fragments, like a fingerprint. [M9]
  > SCENE: lab furnace, sample vaporising, fingerprint pattern on a screen
DOC: The problem? Some fragments from ordinary body fat look a lot like fragments from plastic. [M9]
  > SCENE: two fingerprints overlay almost perfectly: FAT vs PLASTIC
DOC: Take out the water, and the brain is about sixty percent fat. [M9]
  > SCENE: brain with a big "60% FAT" stamp
DOC: ⚡ In other words, some of that plastic... might not be plastic at all. [M9]
DOC: One analysis listed eighteen studies that didn't properly account for these false positives. Scientists have now formally challenged at least seven high-profile papers, including the brain study, the artery study and the bottled water study. [M9, M10]
  > SCENE: papers on a wall get red question marks one by one
DOC: Some critics even suggest that rising obesity, more fat in the body, could explain part of the jump in brain plastic. [M10]
DOC: The original researchers push back. They say the critics are speculating without data of their own, though they admit their methods can still be improved. [M10]
  > SCENE: two sides of a table, scientists arguing (generic avatars)
DOC: One of the scientists behind the blood study says she's still convinced the plastic is there. But the true amount could be half what they measured, or ten times more. [M10]
  > SCENE: a gauge needle swinging wildly between ½× and 10×
DOC: Critics say quite a few papers, in the rush to publish, skipped basic lab checks, like running blank samples to catch contamination. [M10]
  > SCENE: empty test tube labelled BLANK lights up with contamination specks
DOC: ⚡ So is the whole microplastics scare just hype? Not so fast. [M11]

## CHAPTER 5: WHAT'S ACTUALLY TRUE

DOC: Even one of the harshest critics says it's safe to assume plastic is inside us. The fight is over how much, and what it's doing. [M11]
  > SCENE: split screen, both camps nodding at a glowing particle
DOC: And in October, a study in Nature found that tiny plastic in water isn't as harmless as we thought. Its surface can produce aggressive molecules that can damage cells. [M14]
  > SCENE: nanoplastic particle in water sparking reactive radicals
DOC: That was in the lab, not in people. But it's a possible way plastic could do real damage inside the body. [M14]
DOC: And plastic rarely travels alone. Many plastics contain added chemicals, like BPA and phthalates, which pediatricians warn can interfere with hormones. [M18]
  > SCENE: plastic particle with chemical tags orbiting it, hormone icons flickering
DOC: Governments are starting to move. The EPA has, for the first time, proposed adding microplastics to its list of drinking water contaminants it may one day regulate. And the U.S. health department is spending a hundred and forty four million dollars to study the risks. [M15, M16]
  > SCENE: EPA-style list (no logo) with MICROPLASTICS highlighted; money stack
DOC: Meanwhile, some clinics already charge thousands to clean plastic out of your blood, before anyone knows how much is really there. [M13]
  > SCENE: glossy clinic ad, price tag, red question mark
DOC: And the global treaty meant to end plastic pollution is still stuck, with countries split over whether to limit production. [M17]
  > SCENE: conference table, empty chairs, gavel frozen
DOC: ⚡ Which leaves you in the strangest possible place. [op]

## OUTRO

DOC: The scary headlines may have overstated how much plastic is in your brain. And shaky studies could even help the plastic industry wave the whole problem away. [M10, M12]
  > SCENE: headline clippings shrinking; a corporate hand sweeping them off the table
DOC: But plastic is almost certainly inside you, it's in the water you drink, and production keeps climbing. [M5, M6, M4, M1]
  > SCENE: pull back from a bloodstream to a city to the planet wrapped in plastic haze
DOC: Pediatricians already suggest a few cheap precautions: don't microwave food in plastic when you can avoid it, and keep plastic out of the dishwasher. Heat makes it shed chemicals. [M18]
  > SCENE: kitchen, avatar swaps a plastic tub for glass
DOC: The real question isn't whether there's plastic in you. It's how long we keep making more of it, before we find out what it's doing. [op]
  > SCENE: slow push into a single glowing particle, cut to black
DOC: So be honest: are you checking your water bottle right now? [op]
  > SCENE: end card, Doc in the lab, subscribe gear
