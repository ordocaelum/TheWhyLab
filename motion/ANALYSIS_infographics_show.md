# Teardown: The Infographics Show, "Karma Just Hit Roblox. Hard." (14:54)

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpheDQ5v6oQ (15.5M subs; ~86K views within 11 h; analysed 2026-10-25 from the transcript + sampled frames)

## The surprise: visuals are *simpler* than ours
Flat vector, mostly white/blue backgrounds, one generic suited presenter pointing, big bold stat cards ("30 ARRESTS since 2018", "338+ MILLION IMAGES", "MARKETPLACE FEE"). No lighting, no depth, no sets. **The video works because of the script and the topic, not the art.** We've been over-investing in fidelity and under-investing in story.

## What the script does
1. **Contrarian, high-stakes cold open (0:00–0:30).** "Most people think Roblox is a harmless playground for kids… it's not." Then: sinister, addictive, financial machine, reckoning, "the game you know… is dead." The cold open promises a story with a villain and a collapse, and it doesn't answer anything yet.
2. **A villain and a victim.** The platform ("empire", "the house always wins") vs. kids and underpaid young developers. Every fact is framed as part of that conflict.
3. **An escalation ladder.** Each section raises the stakes: harmless platform → child safety → scams → *the platform itself is the scam* → lawsuits → age checks → your face and ID on their servers → stock collapse → verdict. It never plateaus.
4. **A cliffhanger line every ~30–40 s.** About 25 one-sentence turns in 15 minutes: "And that's why the empire is collapsing." / "But was it enough?" / "But the scams in the game are nothing compared to the scam being run by the platform." / "That opened up a whole new can of worms." / "So why are so many people so angry?" / "They didn't have a choice." / "And now it's catching up with them." Each one ends a beat and opens the next question.
5. **Concrete numbers that hit the viewer.** 85M daily users, half of kids under 16, 40% under 13, 30,000 Robux = $114, 30% fee, 4× markup, $57M in settlements, −22% in one night. Money, kids and personal data: things the viewer feels.
6. **Timely.** Built on this year's news (2026 lawsuits, age verification, stock slide, FTC complaint).
7. **Verdict and next-video hook.** It closes with a judgement ("based on exploitation") and an immediate click to a related story.
8. **Narrator only, no banter.** Tone is a confident, slightly ominous storyteller.

## Why the moon episode felt bland by comparison
- **No stakes:** nothing to lose, nobody wronged, no money, no danger to *you*.
- **Answer given at 0:20:** after that there's no open question pulling the viewer forward.
- **Explanatory transitions, not cliffhangers:** "Let's start with…", "People have noticed this for a long time."
- **Facts without consequences:** half a degree, 1.5% farther. True, but nothing rides on them.
- **Our depth went into visuals** (sets, lighting) instead of into the story.

## Rules to adopt (feed into RESEARCH.md §1 and the script step)
- **Topic score adds three criteria:** *Conflict* (is there a villain, a cover-up, a fight?), *Stakes for the viewer* (money, health, safety, kids, privacy), *Escalation* (can it get worse 4–5 times?). Science "why" topics must be framed through a stake: "Why the flu keeps beating your vaccine", not "How flu viruses mutate".
- **Cold open:** a contrarian claim, stakes, and a promise. Never the answer.
- **Hook line every 30–40 s:** a one-sentence turn that closes the beat and opens a question. Budget ~1 per 90 words.
- **Escalation ladder outline:** each chapter must raise stakes over the last. Mid-point turn ("but the real problem is…").
- **Numbers with consequences:** every stat answers "so what does it cost me / who gets hurt?"
- **End with a verdict + next-video hook.**
- **Production:** keep our look, but allow fast "stat card" scenes (big number + icon + motion). Spend craft on the 5–6 money shots, not on every line.
